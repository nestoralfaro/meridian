## One number would be misleading

Nobody knows what markets will return next year, let alone over thirty years. A calculator that assumes a steady 6% a year gives one tidy answer, and it's almost certainly wrong.

## Many possible futures

A **Monte Carlo** projection simulates thousands of possible market histories. Each year of each history draws a return at random, around a long-run average and with realistic ups and downs. For each history, the planner adds your savings until retirement, then takes out your yearly spending.

## Reading the result

- **"82% chance your savings last to 95"** means that in 82% of the simulated histories, money was left at 95.
- The **shaded band** in the chart covers the middle 80% of outcomes, from the 10th to the 90th percentile; the line is the middle outcome.
- Results are in **today's dollars**: the model works after inflation, so $40,000 of spending means today's buying power.

## What it can't tell you

The assumptions are simple: average returns, typical volatility, and no taxes or fees. Use the result to compare choices, such as saving more, retiring later or spending less, rather than as a forecast.
